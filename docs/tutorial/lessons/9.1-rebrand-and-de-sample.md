# Lesson 9.1 — Rebrand & de-sample

> **Part 9 · Make it yours.** You've rebuilt an entire multi-tenant SaaS platform from an empty
> solution — every line understood, every decision examined. But it's still *Perezosoft*, and it
> still has a `Notes` sample slice sitting in it. This final lesson turns the reference platform
> into **your product**: replace every brand touchpoint (there are more than you'd guess, and the
> most-missed one is the transactional email), delete the demo slice cleanly, and rename the
> tenant concept to your domain's word. It's a short lesson with a serious point — a rebrand that
> *looks* done in the UI but ships emails with someone else's logo is a rebrand that isn't done,
> and the way you make "done" *verifiable* is the real lesson.

**Goal:** every occurrence of the reference brand (name, tagline, logo, colours, app id, OAuth
scheme) replaced with yours — including the inline email templates and every localization file;
the `Notes` sample slice removed with no dangling references; the tenant term renamed to your
domain's word; and a mechanical check that proves the rebrand is complete.

**Concepts & principles:** brand touchpoints as a checklist (not a vibe); why inline email is the
most-missed spot; a completeness backstop (`git grep`); the sample slice as scaffolding to delete;
domain naming (the tenant term); scope discipline (the OUT list); making "done" verifiable.

**Maps to:** `docs/REBRANDING.md` · repo: `src/Infrastructure/Email/BrandedEmail.cs`,
`src/Infrastructure/Email/Assets/logo.png`, `src/Shared.Ui/wwwroot/brand/*`,
`src/Api/Features/Notes/*` (to delete), `docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md` (the OUT list).

**Prerequisites:** 2.3 (`IEmailSender` + the branded email), 3.2 (the Notes slice you're now
deleting), 2.8 (the tenant/`Household` concept you're renaming), 3.5 (localization / the `.resx`
files that also carry the brand).

---

## 1. Motivate — "looks rebranded" is not "is rebranded"

You change the header wordmark, the login page, the favicon. The app looks like yours. Then a user
signs up, gets an OTP email — and it arrives with *Perezosoft's* logo, green palette, and tagline
in the footer. Because the transactional emails are a **completely separate rendering path** from
the app: email HTML can't load your app's CSS or static assets (mail clients strip `<link>`, block
data-URIs, and ignore web fonts), so the branded email carries its *own* inline copy of the name,
colours, logo, and tagline. It's the single most-forgotten surface in any rebrand, and it's the one
your customers see in their inbox — arguably a *more* prominent brand impression than the app
chrome. The lesson's real subject is therefore not "how to change a colour" but **how to know
you're actually done** across a surface area larger than it looks.

## 2. The checklist — every touchpoint, grouped

`docs/REBRANDING.md` enumerates the touchpoints so none are missed. They cluster into six groups:

1. **Name & wordmark** — the header (`AppHeader.razor`), login/home headings, the web + MAUI
   `index.html` `<title>`s, the OAuth "you can close this tab" page, **`BrandedEmail.cs`'s footer**,
   *and every localization `.resx`* (`AppStrings.*.resx`, `EmailStrings.*.resx` — the brand name
   lives in the copy, in every language you ship), plus the email sender name (`Email:Smtp:FromName`
   in appsettings + any env override).
2. **Tagline** — the SVG lockup and, again, `BrandedEmail.cs`.
3. **Logos & images** — the RCL brand assets (SVG source + the PNG lockups the UI actually
   references — see §3), the web favicons/OG image/PWA icons, the MAUI launcher icon + splash, and
   **the CID-embedded email logo** (`Infrastructure/Email/Assets/logo.png` — kept a PNG because mail
   clients strip SVG).
4. **Colour palette** — the semantic `:root` tokens in `app.css` (single-sourced for web *and*
   native), and the **hard-coded colour constants at the top of `BrandedEmail.cs`** (email can't use
   CSS variables, so the palette is duplicated there — rename them while you're in the file).
5. **App identifier & OAuth callback scheme** — the MAUI `CallbackScheme`, Android intent-filter,
   both Apple `Info.plist` `CFBundleURLSchemes`, the API's `Auth:Native:CallbackScheme`, the
   `ApplicationId`, *and* your OAuth provider console registrations — all must agree.
6. **Marketing/store assets** — LinkedIn/app-store icons (not shipped in-app).

Notice how many groups end with *"and `BrandedEmail.cs`."* The email template appears in **four**
of the six — name, tagline, logo, palette — precisely because it's a self-contained brand artifact.
That repetition in the checklist is a deliberate nudge: the checklist is engineered so the
most-missed surface is the hardest to skip.

## 3. Two gotchas the checklist encodes

Beyond the enumeration, two non-obvious traps are worth internalizing because they *look* fine and
silently aren't:

- **The UI references the PNG lockups, not the SVGs.** A wordmark SVG embedded via `<img>` won't
  load your webfont, so its text silently falls back to Helvetica/Arial — your logo, wrong typeface,
  and no error. So the SVGs are the *editable source*, the PNGs are *rendered from them*, and
  `Login.razor`/`Home.razor` point at the **PNGs**. Replace the SVG, re-render the PNG, or the logo
  looks subtly broken.
- **The email logo must be a PNG, CID-embedded.** `BrandedEmail.cs` embeds the logo via
  `cid:perezosoft-logo` (multipart/related) — the one approach Gmail *and* Outlook render reliably;
  they block data-URIs and strip SVG. Swap `Assets/logo.png` (a ~128px square), keep the filename to
  avoid touching references.

These are the kind of details that turn a "quick rebrand" into an afternoon of confusion — which is
exactly why they live in a written checklist, not in someone's memory.

## 4. The completeness backstop — make "done" a command

Here's the lesson's sharpest idea. After working the checklist, you don't *hope* it's complete —
you **prove** it:

```sh
git grep -i perezosoft        # → nothing (outside docs/REBRANDING.md itself)
git grep -i "Lazy reputation. Efficient engineering."   # the tagline → nothing
```

If either returns a hit, you missed a spot, and it tells you exactly where. This turns "is the
rebrand done?" from a subjective judgment into a **mechanical, repeatable check** — the same instinct
as the coverage gate, the arch tests, and the RLS parity gate throughout this course: *don't rely on
diligence when a command can verify.* Pair it with a functional check the grep can't do — trigger a
real OTP or invite email and confirm it arrives with your logo, colours, name, and tagline — and
"done" is now two concrete, falsifiable steps instead of a vibe. A rebrand you can *verify* is a
rebrand you can *trust*.

## 5. De-sample — delete the `Notes` slice cleanly

The `Notes` feature (3.2) was scaffolding — the reference vertical slice that taught the pattern.
Your product replaces it with real features, so it goes. Deleting it cleanly means removing the
whole slice *and* its wiring, in one commit:

- the `src/Api/Features/Notes/` directory (handler, endpoints, models, `NotesDataContributor`);
- its two `Program.cs` lines (`AddScoped<NotesHandler>` + the contributor registration, and
  `app.MapNotes()`) — flagged with `🗑️ DELETE-ME` comments precisely so they're easy to find;
- the `AddNotesSample` migration (or leave it — it's applied history; for a fresh product, squash);
- the `NotesSliceTests`.

The `🗑️ DELETE-ME` markers are the reference platform doing you a courtesy: the sample was built to
be *removed*, so its every touchpoint is signposted. After deleting, the build + arch tests + the
coverage gate all still pass — which is the proof the removal was clean (no dangling reference, no
orphaned registration). Then you build your first *real* slice by copying the pattern Notes taught
(the CLAUDE.md instruction: "copy `Features/Notes` as the reference, then delete the Notes sample").

## 6. Rename the tenant term — speak your domain

The platform's tenant concept ships with the app-facing label **Household** (`/api/household`,
`HouseholdController`) — but your domain has its own word: *Workspace*, *Team*, *Organization*,
*Practice*, *Band*. Renaming it (routes, controller, UI strings, the `.resx` copy) makes the product
speak your users' language. Crucially, the *code* term stays **tenant** (the internal abstraction is
domain-agnostic on purpose) — you're renaming the *app-facing label*, not the architectural concept.
That separation is why the rename is a surface change, not a refactor: `ITenantScoped`, the query
filter, and RLS never mention "Household," so they never need to know you now call it a "Workspace."
The abstraction earning its keep one final time.

## 7. Scope discipline — the OUT list

One more habit for making it yours: before building a feature, check the **"OUT" list in
`docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`**. Every project accumulates "wouldn't it be nice" ideas; a written
*out-of-scope* list is how you keep the build focused and how you make deferral a *decision* rather
than a drift. Adding something the brief explicitly deferred should require an explicit change to the
brief — the same "don't silently reverse a settled decision" discipline as the ADR log (0.1). The
platform models this: features like per-record ACLs (6.1) and the non-web framework commitment are
*named as deferred*, not forgotten. Making it yours means keeping that discipline as you extend.

## 8. Red — the rebrand is verifiable

```gherkin
Scenario: no reference brand survives
  When I run `git grep -i perezosoft` and the tagline search
  Then both return nothing (outside docs/REBRANDING.md)

Scenario: a real email carries the new brand
  When I trigger an OTP or invite email
  Then it arrives with the new logo (CID PNG), palette, name, and tagline

Scenario: the sample is gone and the build is still clean
  When Features/Notes and its wiring are deleted
  Then build + arch tests + coverage gate all pass (no dangling reference)
```

There's no xUnit here — the "tests" are a grep, an inbox, and a green build. That's fitting: the
lesson is about *verifiable completeness* of a cross-cutting change, and the tools that verify it are
the mechanical checks, not assertions in a test project.

## 9. Run it

```sh

```

## 10. Architecture Decision

> **The fork:** how is the reference platform turned into a distinct product, completely and
> verifiably? (a) Change what's visible in the running app and call it done; (b) a find-and-replace
> of the brand string; (c) a written, grouped checklist of every touchpoint (incl. the separate
> email-rendering path and every localization file), backed by a `git grep` completeness gate + a
> real-email functional check, plus a signposted sample-slice deletion.
>
> **Chosen:** (c) (`docs/REBRANDING.md`). Branding spans surfaces that don't share code (app CSS vs
> inline email vs native manifests vs `.resx` copy vs OAuth registrations), so only an enumerated
> checklist covers them; the `git grep` backstop makes completeness *mechanical*; the real-email
> check catches the most-missed surface functionally; the `🗑️ DELETE-ME` markers make de-sampling a
> clean, verifiable removal.
>
> **Rejected:** (a) — "looks rebranded" ships someone else's logo in every transactional email, the
> exact §1 failure. (b) — a blind find-and-replace misses the logo binaries, the colour constants,
> the app-id/scheme agreement across five files + the provider console, and would clumsily rewrite
> the *code* term `tenant` too; branding isn't only strings.
>
> **The trade:** a checklist is manual work and must be *maintained* as new brand surfaces are added
> (a new email template, a new native platform). Accepted: the `git grep` gate catches a
> stale/incomplete checklist (a missed surface shows up as a grep hit), so the manual list is itself
> backstopped — the same defense-in-depth instinct as the rest of the platform, applied to branding.

## 11. Checkpoint

```sh
git add -A && git commit -m "chore: rebrand to <YourProduct>, delete the Notes sample, rename the tenant label"
git tag lesson-9.1
```
